Fall

by Scott Mann · Thriller

A year after witnessing her husband Dan plummet to his death in a rock-climbing accident, a grief-stricken Becky is convinced by her thrill-seeking best friend, Hunter, to scale the abandoned B67 TV tower in the Mojave Desert to scatter Dan's ashes and find closure. They successfully reach the miniature platform at the very top, but as they begin their descent, the corroded ladder detaches completely, marooning them thousands of feet in the air without water or a communication signal. As days pass, the women face dehydration, predatory vultures, and a devastating emotional fracture when Becky realizes Hunter had a secret affair with Dan before his death. In a desperate move to retrieve their backpack from a satellite dish lower down the structure, Hunter climbs down but falls; though Becky seemingly pulls her back up, a shocking psychological twist reveals that Hunter actually died in the drop and Becky has been hallucinating her companion due to delirium. Pushed to her absolute survival limits, Becky kills an attacking vulture for sustenance, climbs down to Hunter's physical corpse, and stuffs her cell phone into Hunter's shoe—dropping the body to the ground so the shoe can shield the phone until it falls into cellular range and successfully transmits a distress text. This final act of grim ingenuity allows Becky to be rescued and finally reunite with her worried father.

127 Hours

by Danny Boyle · Thriller

The story begins in April 2003 as thrill-seeker Aron Ralston sets off on a solo weekend hiking trip through Utah's Bluejohn Canyon without informing anyone of his plans. After crossing paths with and guiding two lost hikers, Kristi and Megan, Aron continues deeper into the secluded crevices until he slips, causing a massive boulder to dislodge and permanently wedge his right forearm against the canyon wall. Over the course of the next five days, Aron is pushed to his absolute psychological and physical limits as he severely rations a tiny supply of water, battles hypothermia and dehydration, and records a poignant video diary on his camcorder intended for his family. Plagued by vivid, desperate hallucinations of his past relationships and unlived future, he realizes that no rescue party is coming and that he will die in the canyon. In a final, agonizing act of sheer human will on the sixth day, Aron uses his cheap, dull multi-tool pocket knife to intentionally break his own forearm bones and amputate his trapped arm. Bleeding profusely, he wraps his stump, rappels down a 65-foot cliff wall, and hikes out into the desert heat until he is spotted by a vacationing family and ultimately rescued by a helicopter.

Curve

by Tim Egan · Thriller

The film opens with an unnamed woman waking up from unconsciousness on the steep, curved slope of a mysterious concrete structure, with her leg folded awkwardly beneath her. As she attempts to move, she immediately realizes the terrifying gravity of her situation: the smooth concrete provides almost no friction, and directly below her is a pitch-black, seemingly sentient void that echoes with the distant screams of previous victims. Across the chasm, she notices ominous, dried blood streaks that track downward, confirming that others have tried and failed to climb to safety. Desperately trying to move upward, every micro-movement causes her to painfully scrape her hands and slide closer to the edge. Her struggle intensifies drastically when a rainstorm begins, lubricating the concrete surface and causing her to lose her footing entirely. In a final, agonizing act of survival, she rips off her metal necklace and wraps it around her hand to scratch into the concrete for leverage. The film ends on a hauntingly ambiguous note, cutting to a shot of the now-empty concrete curve with only the sound of rain, leaving the viewer to decide whether she managed to hold on or finally plummeted into the abyss.
